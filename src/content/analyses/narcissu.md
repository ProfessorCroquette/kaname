*Narcissu* (Kataoka Tomo, 2005) follows two terminally ill patients who leave a hospice ward and travel together for fifteen days. One of them, Setsumi, dies at the end, and the work itself classes her death as a suicide. This article asks whether her final act can be read as an exercise of freedom rather than a flight from suffering.

## Constriction and the Closed Situation

It first describes her situation. Shneidman’s account of suicidal states, with its psychache, thwarted needs, constricted thinking and ambivalence, shows how the hospice imposes the two-option choice that his model locates in the suicidal mind.

## Liminality

Turner’s account of liminality shows the ward as a heteronomous threshold of stripped status and enforced obedience, and the road as a self-directed passage that never reaches reincorporation. 

I argue that constriction and the closed situation are one phenomenon at two levels, psychological and structural.
