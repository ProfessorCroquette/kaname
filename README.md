

# 要 Kaname: Core Concept

> [!abstract] One-line pitch A learning project with two halves that hold each other up: **building a website** and **writing philosophical analyses of media** (anime, music, games, film, series), each grounded in cited books and articles.

_Kaname_ (要) means "pivot" or "keystone." The code is the structure, the ideas are the load it carries.

---

## 1. The Core Claim

> [!question] What does Kaname believe? Media can be read as philosophy in practice. A work does not have to _cite_ a thinker to _enact_ an idea. The job is to show **where** and **how**, and to be honest about **where the lens fails**.

## 3. The Expander: Questions That Grow a Concept

Use this section to take any seed idea and push it outward. Copy the block into a new note for each analysis.

### Seed

- **Work:**
- **Medium:** #anime / #music / #game / #film / #series
- **The tension or theme I noticed:**

### Expand it with these prompts

1. **What is the work actually asking?** Phrase the central tension as a question, not a topic.
2. **Which thinkers have wrestled with this question?** List 2 to 3 candidates, not just one.
3. **Where does the work _show_ the idea, not just mention it?** Scenes, lyrics, mechanics, structure, pacing, silence.
4. **Does the medium itself matter?** A game makes you _complicit_, a song compresses, a series accumulates. How does form carry the idea?
5. **What would the creator say?** Check interviews and official statements. Intent is not the final word, but it should be known.
6. **Where does the lens break?** What in the work resists or contradicts the framework?
7. **What is the strongest rival reading?** Argue against yourself.
8. **So what?** What does the philosophy gain from the work, and the work from the philosophy?

> [!warning] The name-dropping trap "This show is like Nietzsche" is not analysis. Analysis names the specific concept, points to the specific moment, and explains the mapping.

## 7. Citation Rules

- [ ] Every philosophical claim points to a **primary text** or a credible **secondary source**.
- [ ] Every claim about the media points to a **specific moment** (episode, track and line, chapter, scene).
- [ ] Use one citation style consistently (pick: APA / MLA / Chicago).
- [ ] Record the **edition and translator** for translated works.
- [ ] Mark unverified citations with `#verify` until checked.
- [ ] Keep a running [[Kaname Bibliography]] note.

> [!bug] Be careful with AI-generated references AI tools can invent plausible-looking citations. Treat any citation you did not personally confirm as unverified.
